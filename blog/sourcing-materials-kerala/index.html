The architect's guide to sourcing building materials in Kerala

Structa Global Technical TeamPublished 1 March 2025Updated 19 October 2026

Key points

  • Define performance requirements before choosing products.
  • Judge the supplier's documentation and technical capability, not only the product.
  • Verify original, current certificates for the exact product.
  • Plan lead times at specification: 2 to 5 days stocked, 8 to 16 weeks custom.

Sourcing decisions made at specification stage have larger consequences than ever: tighter programmes, stricter green building requirements and higher performance expectations. This guide sets out the sequence we recommend to architects, project managers and procurement teams in Kerala.

Stage 1: Define performance before products

The most common error is to choose a product and then fit the specification around it. Define the requirement first, then find the products that meet it. For each material category, the brief should cover:

  • Functional performance: structural capacity, thermal U-value, acoustic reduction, fire resistance and weather tightness.
  • Durability: design life, maintenance regime and performance under coastal exposure, monsoon and UV.
  • Certification: ISO, CE, BIS and ASTM as applicable, plus the green building credits targeted.
  • Appearance: colour, texture, reflectivity and finish.
  • Sustainability: recycled content, embodied carbon, FSC certification and EPD availability.
  • Commercial terms: budget range, required lead time and minimum quantities.

The specification drives the sourcing, not the other way round.

Stage 2: Evaluate the supplier, not only the product

A certified product from an unreliable distributor carries the same project risk as a poor product from a reliable one. Check that the supplier has:

  • ISO 9001:2015 certification covering procurement, testing, storage and delivery.
  • Technical capability to review drawings and recommend products against your performance requirements.
  • Complete documentation for every product: mill certificates, test reports, EPDs, CE Declarations of Performance and BIS compliance.
  • Honest lead times with real visibility of the supply chain.
  • Regional experience of Kochi's coastal exposure, Thrissur's rainfall and the conditions of each market.

Stage 3: Verify certifications

  • Ask for the original certificate, not a copy or a website reference.
  • Check that it is current and covers the exact product and grade being specified.
  • For CE-marked products, ask for the Declaration of Performance, not just the mark.
  • For IGBC or LEED contributions, ask for the product data that supports each credit.
  • For BIS certification, confirm the IS code number applies to the specified product.

Stage 4: Plan the supply timeline at specification stage

Late material delivery is one of the most common and most avoidable causes of programme delay. Typical lead times in Kerala are:

Product typeTypical lead time
Stocked standard products2 to 5 working days in Kochi and Ernakulam; 3 to 7 elsewhere in Kerala
Imported standard products4 to 8 weeks, depending on origin and shipping schedule
Custom specifications8 to 16 weeks, including production, testing and shipping
Large-volume ordersPhased deliveries planned with our logistics team at quotation

Stage 5: Treat the supplier as part of the design team

Projects that finish on time, to specification and within budget usually share one trait: a supplier brought in early and treated as a project partner. We engage at specification, advise through procurement and support delivery and installation. Our involvement ends when the building performs as specified.

Frequently asked questions

What is the typical lead time for architectural materials in Kerala?

Stocked products take 2 to 5 working days in Kochi and Ernakulam and 3 to 7 elsewhere in Kerala. Imported standard products take 4 to 8 weeks, and custom specifications 8 to 16 weeks.

What documents should a building materials supplier provide?

Mill certificates, accredited test reports, CE Declarations of Performance, BIS compliance certificates, Environmental Product Declarations, safety data sheets and warranty documents.

When should a materials supplier be involved in a project?

At concept or early design stage, before the specification is fixed, so performance criteria and lead times can shape the design rather than constrain it.
